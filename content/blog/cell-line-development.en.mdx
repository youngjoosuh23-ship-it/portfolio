## Why does drug development take over 10 years?

Bringing a single new drug from discovery to a patient takes an average of 10–15 years and enormous cost.

Drug development broadly moves through **candidate discovery → preclinical → clinical trials (Phase 1–3) → approval and commercialization**. The bottleneck that trips things up early on is the **Cell Line Development (CLD)** stage.

## What is CLD, and why does it take so long?

To make a biologic (protein therapeutic, antibody therapeutic, etc.), you need to find and grow the "optimal cell" — a cell line that stably secretes the target therapeutic protein at scale.

- **A complex screening process**: out of thousands to tens of thousands of candidate cells, you have to isolate the single cell with high, stable expression.
- **The main source of delay**: the biological growth-rate ceiling of cells, stability & quality testing, and scale-up together consume anywhere from several months to over a year.
- **Risk**: a poor early cell line selection carries a high risk of failure later, at the clinical or mass-production stage.
